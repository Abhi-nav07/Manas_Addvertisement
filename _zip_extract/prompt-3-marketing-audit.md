# Marketing & Conversion Audit — Manas Advertising

This is analysis, not code changes (per the brief's own instruction: "your responsibility is NOT writing code"). Grounded in the actual copy/content already in the repo, not generic filler.

## 1. What's already working (leave alone)
- **Service descriptions are genuinely good.** They're outcome-focused, not activity-lists ("Custom-designed, high-performance websites... engineered for speed, accessibility, and conversion" — this already answers "what/why/trust" in one line). No rewrite needed.
- **Portfolio has a real proof point**: the Saffron Spice Co. case study cites "34% increase in shelf pickup rate within 3 months" — this is exactly the kind of authentic, specific credibility signal the brief asks for. Worth surfacing more prominently (see #4).
- **FAQ answers real objections** (project timelines, startup vs. established clients, retainer support) rather than generic filler — this already does the "reduce uncertainty" job well.
- **Careers page is honest** ("no open roles right now") rather than a fake careers funnel — good trust signal, don't "improve" this into something misleading.

## 2. Needs your judgment call (I won't silently change these)
- **"40+ Industry Awards" in the Hero stats** — I have no way to verify if this is real. The brief explicitly says "avoid fake awards / fabricated statistics," but this is also flagged as existing "core content" I'm not to change unilaterally. Recommendation: either name the actual awards somewhere (About page or a footer badge strip) to back the number up, or soften it if it can't be substantiated. I won't guess which.
- **Testimonials** (Ritika Malhotra, Arjun Sethi, etc.) — read as plausible and specific, not templated ("great work!" style), which is good. I can't verify authenticity from the repo alone — worth confirming these are real clients who consented to being quoted, since a review board / procurement team will take these at face value.

## 3. Concrete, safe improvements (content-level, not redesign)
- **Hero CTA pairing**: currently a strong primary CTA + outline secondary — good pattern, keep. Consider making the secondary CTA text outcome-specific rather than generic (e.g. "See our work" instead of a vaguer label) if it currently isn't — worth a copy pass by whoever owns brand voice, since I don't want to rewrite CTA copy without seeing how it reads with your intended tone.
- **About page** already answers "who/experience/why different" reasonably — the Mumbai/Indore contradiction I fixed earlier was actively undermining trust here (a visitor cross-referencing the address would have caught it). That fix alone measurably helps credibility.
- **Gallery** now shows only the real 6 projects (fixed the earlier duplication bug) — this is more honest and avoids the "random photo dump" feeling the brief warns against, since duplicate captions read as sloppy/careless to a discerning visitor.
- **Local business signal**: the real Indore address, phone numbers, and business hours are now in structured data (JSON-LD), which helps both local SEO and the "is this a real, findable business" trust signal for procurement-type visitors doing due diligence.

## 4. Recommendations I'd flag for a content/marketing owner (not implementing myself)
- Surface the Saffron Spice Co. **34% metric** on the homepage or a "Results" callout — right now it's buried inside the individual case study page. A specific, real number like that is rare and valuable social proof; it's underused.
- Consider adding a one-line "why Indore, why this matters" note if geography is a differentiator (e.g. serving Central India / MP market) — currently the site doesn't make a case for local positioning beyond the address itself.
- FAQ could add one more entry directly addressing "how do we know you're legitimate" — pricing transparency or a simple "what you get in the first call" — since procurement-type visitors specifically look for this before an enterprise inquiry.

## 5. What I'm explicitly not doing
- Not rewriting hero/service headlines wholesale — they already meet the brief's own bar ("build trust / explain value / reduce uncertainty / encourage action").
- Not inventing client logos, certifications, or additional testimonials — the brief explicitly forbids this and I have no real data to add.
- Not changing brand colors, even though the accent-gold contrast issue (flagged in the Prompt 1 report) technically undermines "premium, accessible" positioning — that's your call, not mine.

**Bottom line**: the copy foundation here is stronger than a typical template site — the main gap isn't weak writing, it's a couple of unverifiable claims (award count) and underused proof points (the 34% stat) that a marketing owner should resolve with real data, not more AI-generated copy.
